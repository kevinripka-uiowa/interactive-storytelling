Tutorial M5C

Build a Migration Game in Twine

Create a short interactive story in which a warbler must conserve enough energy to complete its spring migration.

You will use Twine’s Chapbook story format to connect passages, remember choices with one variable, and send the player to different endings.

This story uses a gauntlet structure. The player makes a choice, experiences a consequence, and returns to the main route. The paths repeatedly branch and then merge, keeping the project manageable.

What you will build

A compact bird-migration game with three decisions, an energy score, and three possible endings.

What you will submit

The published HTML story and a screenshot of the complete Twine story map.

Video walkthrough

Video will be added after the tutorial is recorded.
This written tutorial contains the complete build.

Section 1: Create the story

Use the desktop version of Twine and explicitly select Chapbook before adding content.

Start a new Chapbook story

Step 1
  1. Open the desktop Twine application.
  2. Under the Story toolbar tab, select New.
  3. Name the story Survive Migration.
  4. Open the story settings and choose Chapbook as the story format.
  5. Open the starting passage and rename it Start.
Story formats use different syntax

This tutorial uses Chapbook. Harlowe or SugarCube examples found online may look similar, but their variable and conditional code will not necessarily work in this project.

Section 2: Plan the structure

Keep the branching small enough to build, test, and revise during one class period.

Use branches and bottlenecks

Step 2

The story follows this route:

Start
  ↓
Departure decision
  ↙          ↘
Cross Gulf   Follow coast
  ↘          ↙
Stopover decision
  ↙          ↘
Rest         Keep flying
  ↘          ↙
Final push decision
  ↙          ↘
Night flight Day flight
       ↓
Evaluate energy
  ↙      ↓       ↘
Strong   Late    Exhausted
Branching does not require endless branches

Each decision creates a short divergence, but the routes merge at a bottleneck before the next decision. The player experiences consequences without forcing you to write dozens of unique paths.

Section 3: Build the opening

Initialize the energy variable and explain the player’s role.

Add the Start passage

Step 3

Open the Start passage and enter:

energy: 100
config.header.right: "Energy: {energy}"
--
# Survive Migration

You are a migratory warbler preparing to migrate north. Your destination is still hundreds of miles away, and every choice will affect the energy you have left.

Reach the breeding grounds with as much energy as possible.

[[Begin the journey->Departure]]
The vars section comes first

In Chapbook, variable assignments appear above the double hyphen. The passage text that the player sees appears below it.

A link can use different display text

The player sees “Begin the journey,” but the link opens a passage named Departure. Twine creates that destination passage automatically when you close the editor.

Section 4: Add the first decision

Create two choices with different costs, then merge both routes into the next decision.

Choose a migration route

Step 4

Open Departure and enter:

# The first major decision

A strong south wind could carry you directly across the Gulf of Mexico. The crossing is shorter, but there is nowhere to rest if the weather changes.

Following the coastline is safer, but it adds hundreds of miles.

[[Cross the Gulf->Cross Gulf]]

[[Follow the coastline->Follow Coast]]

Create the two consequence passages:

Cross Gulf

energy: energy - 35
--
A storm forms during the crossing. You fight the wind for hours before finally reaching land.

[[Search for a stopover->Stopover]]

Follow Coast

energy: energy - 20
--
The coastal route takes longer, but you find favorable winds and avoid the storm over open water.

[[Search for a stopover->Stopover]]
The choice changes state

Both routes continue to the same passage, but they subtract different amounts from energy. That remembered consequence can affect the ending later.

Section 5: Track energy

Add a second decision that lets the player recover energy or risk continuing immediately.

Balance time against recovery

Step 5

Stopover

# A shrinking stopover

You reach a patch of shrubs surrounded by roads and buildings. There are insects here, but stopping will delay your arrival.

[[Rest and feed->Rest]]

[[Keep flying->Keep Flying]]

Rest

energy: energy + 25
--
You spend the day feeding and recover some of the energy lost during the first leg.

[[Continue north->Final Push]]

Keep Flying

energy: energy - 20
--
You continue without stopping. You gain time, but your wingbeats become slower and less efficient.

[[Continue north->Final Push]]
A meaningful choice involves a tradeoff

“Rest” is not simply correct and “keep flying” is not simply wrong. One protects energy; the other protects time. Strong choices ask players to prioritize competing goals.

Section 6: Complete the gauntlet

Add one final decision and route both choices to the shared evaluation passage.

Choose when to make the final flight

Step 6

Final Push

# The final push

The breeding grounds are within reach. You can travel overnight in cooler air or wait until daylight, when navigation is easier but predators are more active.

[[Fly through the night->Night Flight]]

[[Wait for daylight->Day Flight]]

Night Flight

energy: energy - 25
--
You fly through darkness and shifting winds, using the stars and Earth’s magnetic field to stay on course.

[[See how the migration ends->Evaluate]]

Day Flight

energy: energy - 15
--
You wait for daylight. The route is easier to read, though you spend more time avoiding predators.

[[See how the migration ends->Evaluate]]

Section 7: Create conditional endings

Use the final energy value to reveal one of three outcomes.

Evaluate the accumulated consequences

Step 7

Open Evaluate and enter:

[if energy >= 70]
# A strong arrival

You reach the breeding grounds with enough energy to establish a territory quickly.

**Final energy: {energy}**

[else if energy >= 45]
# A late arrival

You complete the migration, but you need time to recover before competing for a territory.

**Final energy: {energy}**

[else]
# An exhausted arrival

You reach the region depleted. The journey is over, but surviving the next few days will still be difficult.

**Final energy: {energy}**
The ending checks the current state

Chapbook reads the conditions from top to bottom. The first true condition displays its section. The final [else] catches any remaining energy value.

Test the thresholds

Play several routes and confirm that each ending is possible. Adjust the costs or thresholds when one ending can never be reached.

Section 8: Test the story

Use Chapbook’s testing tools to inspect variables and check every route.

Test choices, links, and energy

Step 8
  1. Select Test rather than only using Play.
  2. Use Chapbook’s Backstage panel to watch the energy variable change.
  3. Complete at least three playthroughs using different combinations of choices.
  4. Confirm that every passage has a way forward.
  5. Confirm that links lead to the intended passages and that all three endings can appear.
  6. Review the story map for accidental duplicate or disconnected passages.
Test the system, not just the prose

A story can read well and still contain broken links, impossible endings, or incorrect variable changes. Deliberately test the strongest and weakest possible routes.

Section 9: Back up and move the story

Your Twine library belongs to one computer or one browser. Export a file before changing machines.

Do not assume the story follows your login

Step 9

Desktop Twine

The desktop application stores its story library as files on that computer. It does not automatically synchronize the library to another lab computer.

Browser Twine

The online version stores stories in that browser’s local storage. A different browser, browser profile, or computer has a different library. Clearing browser data can erase the stories stored there.

To continue on another computer

  1. In the Story Map, open the Build toolbar tab.
  2. Select Publish to File and save the HTML file somewhere you control, such as OneDrive, Google Drive, or a USB drive.
  3. On the second computer, open Twine.
  4. Under the Library toolbar tab, choose Import.
  5. Select the published HTML file. Twine will add the editable story to that computer’s library.
Publishing also creates a backup

A published Twine HTML file is playable in a browser and can be imported back into Twine for editing. Save a new copy whenever you complete substantial work.

Archive is useful for multiple stories

The Library’s Archive command exports the entire story library. Use it when moving or backing up several Twine projects at once.

Section 10: Publish the game

Create the final self-contained HTML file that can be opened or hosted as a webpage.

Export the finished story

Step 10
  1. Rename the story if its current title is not ready for publication.
  2. Test the complete story one final time.
  3. Open the Build toolbar tab.
  4. Select Publish to File.
  5. Name the file survive-migration.html.
  6. Open the exported file in a browser and complete one more playthrough.
The published file is self-contained

Twine packages the passages, Chapbook format, variables, and styling into one HTML file. The file can be opened locally or uploaded to web hosting.

Submission

Complete
