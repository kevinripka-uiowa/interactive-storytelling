Build a Simple Guided Data Story in Flourish

Create a four-slide story about when warbler species can be seen in Iowa. Each transition has a different storytelling job: revisualize the same data, focus attention, and introduce new evidence.

A guided data story makes an argument in steps. The viewer advances with buttons, while the author controls the sequence and explains why each visual change matters.

What you will build

One Flourish story with four slides: overview, revisualization, focus, and new evidence.

What you will submit

The public URL to the published Flourish story.

Video tutorial

A video will be added after this written tutorial has been tested.

Download the data

This story uses one dataset for the first three slides and a second dataset for the final slide.

Prepare the files

Setup
  1. Download iowa-warbler-monthly-counts.csv.
  2. Download iowa-warbler-seasonal-status.csv.
  3. Sign in to Flourish and open your Projects page.
  4. Keep this tutorial open in another tab.
Tutorial data

These small datasets are invented for practice. They are designed to make the storytelling transitions easy to see.

Plan the four-slide argument

Each slide should add a new idea rather than simply changing the chart.

Define the sequence

Purpose

Slide 1: Overview

A line chart shows two migration peaks and a smaller summer population.

Slide 2: Revisualization

The same monthly data becomes ranked bars so viewers can compare months precisely.

Slide 3: Focus

The chart keeps the same arrangement but emphasizes May and July.

Slide 4: New evidence

A second dataset shows that most species are migration visitors rather than Iowa breeders.

The argument

Warbler variety peaks during migration, falls in summer, and remains lower because only part of the larger migration group breeds in Iowa.

Build the opening view

Start with the broad seasonal pattern.

Slide 1: Create a line chart

Overview
  1. Choose New visualization in Flourish.
  2. Select a line-chart template.
  3. Open the Data tab and import iowa-warbler-monthly-counts.csv.
  4. Bind Month to the x-axis and Warbler species to the numeric values.
  5. Keep the months in chronological order.
  6. Add the title Warbler variety rises during spring and fall migration.
  7. Use a visible line and points. Keep the y-axis starting at zero.

Create the Flourish story

Turn the finished line chart into a story before creating the remaining views.

Add the opening visualization to a story

Story
  1. Choose Create a story from the finished visualization.
  2. Use the line chart as Slide 1.
  3. Add the suggested caption.
  4. Rename the story Iowa Warblers Through the Year.
Use visible story navigation

Flourish stories use buttons or arrows to move through slides. In other tools, the same guided sequence could be triggered by scrolling.

Revisualize the same data

Change the visual form because the question changes.

Slide 2: Change the line chart into ranked bars

Revisualization
  1. Duplicate Slide 1.
  2. Use Flourish’s revisualization or chart-type controls to change the line chart into horizontal bars.
  3. Sort the bars from highest to lowest.
  4. Show value labels at the ends of the bars.
  5. Change the title to May has the most warbler species.

Suggested caption: Ranking the same monthly data makes the comparison exact. May leads with 34 species, followed by September with 31.

Why revisualize?

The line chart shows seasonal shape. The ranked bar chart answers a different question: which months have the most species?

Focus on the key comparison

Keep the chart stable and direct attention to the evidence that supports the next claim.

Slide 3: Emphasize May and July

Focus
  1. Duplicate Slide 2.
  2. Keep the bars in the same ranked order.
  3. Use the accent color for May and July.
  4. Mute the other bars.
  5. Add a short annotation if the template allows it.
  6. Change the title to Species variety drops sharply after spring migration.

Suggested caption: Iowa goes from 34 visible warbler species in May to 12 in July. The focus transition isolates the comparison without making viewers relearn the chart.

Focus is not a new chart

The data and arrangement stay the same. Only emphasis changes, helping the viewer see the subset or data points that matter.

Introduce a new dataset

Add evidence that helps explain the pattern shown in the first dataset.

Build a second visualization

New evidence

  3. Import iowa-warbler-seasonal-status.csv.
  4. Bind Seasonal status to the categories and Warbler species to the values.
  5. Use two simple bars with direct value labels.
  6. Add the title Most Iowa warblers are migration visitors.

  1. Open the existing Iowa Warblers Through the Year story.
  2. Add a new slide.
  3. Select the seasonal-status bar chart as the visualization for Slide 4.
  4. Add the caption below.

Suggested caption: The summer total is lower because only 12 species in this practice dataset breed in Iowa. Another 22 appear mainly during migration.

Why introduce new evidence?

The first dataset establishes what happens through the year. The second dataset helps explain why the summer total is smaller.

Refine and test the story

Make the four slides feel like one connected explanation.

Complete the final checks

5 A’s

Appearance

  • Use one accent color consistently across all slides.
  • Use neutral colors for context.
  • Keep titles and captions short.

All devices and accessibility

  • Preview the story on desktop and mobile.
  • Do not rely only on color. Use titles, captions, values, and annotations.
  • Check contrast and keyboard navigation.
  • Add a text description in Flourish when available.

Attribution

  • Add the source note: Source: invented tutorial data for classroom practice.

Story test

  • Slide 1 establishes the broad pattern.
  • Slide 2 revisualizes the same data for comparison.
  • Slide 3 focuses on two values without changing the arrangement.
  • Slide 4 introduces a separate dataset that explains the pattern.

Submission

Publish the story and submit its public URL.

What to submit

Deliverable
  • One published Flourish story URL.
  • Exactly four story slides.
  • One revisualization using the monthly dataset.
  • One focus slide that preserves the previous chart arrangement.
  • One final slide using the separate seasonal-status dataset.
  • A title, caption, source note, readable labels, and accessible contrast.
