Spatial and Immersive Media

Spatial and immersive media help viewers feel positioned inside a scene or near an object. The story job is usually Experience, but these tools can also Reveal, Explain, Locate, or Compare when the interaction helps viewers inspect details.

Core idea: immersion is not enough by itself. A 360 photo or 3D model needs a reason to be interactive: moving through a place, inspecting evidence, opening annotations, comparing views, understanding scale, or entering a fully immersive VR environment.

360 photos

Use 360 photos when the viewer needs to understand a place from inside the scene. They are strongest when spatial context, atmosphere, or point of view matters.

Simple exploration

A basic 360 viewer lets the viewer pan around a scene. This is the most direct immersive interaction: the viewer controls where to look.

Pan to explore

Story job

Experience. Also supports Locate when the surrounding place matters.

Agency

Open visual inspection. The viewer chooses where to look, but stays inside one scene.

Affordances

Spatial and participatory. The viewer navigates a visual environment rather than reading a fixed frame.

Possible interactions

  • Click/touch and drag to pan horizontally or vertically.
  • Use compass, reset, or home-view controls to reorient the viewer.
  • Limit pan range when a full 360 scene is not needed.

Hotspots and annotations

Hotspots attach explanatory content to locations inside the 360 scene. They turn a scene from simple atmosphere into an inspectable spatial explainer.

Point-based detail

Story job

Experience and Reveal. Also supports Explain when annotations teach what to notice.

Agency

Guided inspection. The viewer can look around freely, then choose which points to open.

Affordances

Spatial, encyclopedic, participatory, and procedural. Details are attached to places and appear when selected.

Possible interactions

  • Click/tap hotspots to open text, photos, audio, video, or source notes.
  • Use tooltip labels on hover/focus to preview what a hotspot contains.
  • Use rich annotations for longer explanation, not just labels.
  • Use reveal-page patterns inside the scene: tooltips, modals, hotspots, and annotation cards.

Linked views and tours

Linked views connect multiple 360 scenes. A tour can be open, where viewers choose their path, or guided, where the author moves them through a planned sequence.

Move between scenes

Story job

Experience. Also supports Locate and Explain when the path clarifies how places connect.

Agency

Open exploration for self-directed tours; guided agency for step-through tours.

Affordances

Spatial, participatory, and procedural. The viewer moves through a structured set of scenes.

Possible interactions

  • Link hotspots from one 360 photo to another.
  • Offer open scene choices such as “marsh,” “woodland,” or “shoreline.”
  • Use guided next/previous controls for a tour with a planned order.
  • Use map thumbnails or scene labels so viewers understand where they are in the tour.

3D objects

Use 3D models when shape, volume, orientation, mechanism, space, or scale matters. A model can represent a single object, but it can also represent a room, building, landscape, exhibit, or other location that viewers need to inspect from more than one angle.

Simple 3D exploration

A basic 3D viewer lets the viewer rotate, zoom, and sometimes move around an object or modeled space. This is useful when a single flat image cannot show the form, layout, or spatial relationship clearly.

Pan, zoom, move

Story job

Experience and Explain. The model can be felt as a form or space and inspected as evidence.

Agency

Direct manipulation. The viewer controls angle, distance, and sometimes position.

Affordances

Spatial, participatory, and procedural. The model responds to viewer input.

Possible interactions

  • Drag to rotate around an object or pan through a modeled space.
  • Zoom in/out to inspect form, surface details, or spatial layout.
  • Move, orbit, or reset the model when the viewer gets disoriented.
  • Constrain rotation or movement if only certain angles or pathways matter.

Hotspots and annotations

3D hotspots attach labels or explanations to features of a model. They work best when the viewer needs to inspect parts, mechanisms, materials, evidence, rooms, landmarks, or locations within a modeled space.

Model annotations

Story job

Explain and Reveal. Also supports Experience because the viewer inspects the model spatially.

Agency

Guided inspection. The viewer controls the model and chooses which labeled part to inspect.

Affordances

Spatial and encyclopedic. The interaction attaches explanatory layers to parts of the object or locations inside the modeled space.

Possible interactions

  • Click/tap labels attached to model parts, rooms, landmarks, or evidence points.
  • Use hover/focus labels for short names and modals for longer explanations.
  • Highlight a part or location when its annotation opens.
  • Pair hotspots with camera moves that rotate or move the model toward the selected feature.

Real-world scale via AR projection

AR projection places a 3D object into a camera view or real-world space. It is useful when the viewer needs to understand size, presence, or how an object fits into an environment.

Scale in place

Story job

Experience and Compare. It helps viewers understand size in relation to a familiar space.

Agency

Embodied exploration. The viewer places or moves the object in their own surroundings.

Affordances

Spatial, participatory, and experiential. The object is understood through scale and placement.

Possible interactions

  • Place a model in a room or outdoor scene using AR.
  • Move, rotate, or resize the model to compare scale.
  • Switch between objects to compare size: sparrow, hawk, heron, eagle.
  • Use a fallback non-AR scale graphic for devices that do not support AR.

Full immersion via VR

Use VR when the viewer should feel placed inside a scene or modeled environment rather than looking at it from the outside. This can extend either 360 photos or 3D models.

Full immersion via VR

Full VR turns spatial media into an embodied experience. A 360 photo can become a headset-based scene, while a 3D model can become a walkable or inspectable space, location, exhibit, or environment.

Enter the scene

Story job

Experience. Also supports Locate and Explain when the viewer needs to understand where things are in relation.

Agency

Embodied exploration. The viewer looks around naturally and may move through a guided or open environment.

Affordances

Spatial, participatory, and experiential. The viewer navigates from inside the scene instead of inspecting a framed image.

Possible interactions

  • Look around with headset movement rather than mouse or touch drag.
  • Move between 360 scenes as a VR tour, either open exploration or a guided route.
  • Walk, teleport, or move through a modeled 3D space such as a room, historic site, habitat, exhibit, or landscape.
  • Use gaze, controller, or pointer-based hotspots to open annotations inside the environment.
  • Use guided stops, narration, captions, or highlighted areas so the experience still has a story path.
