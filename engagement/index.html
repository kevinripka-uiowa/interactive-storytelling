Engagement Techniques

Engagement techniques motivate viewers to begin, continue, and complete an interaction. They are not separate storytelling patterns. They can strengthen quizzes, calculators, branching paths, maps, generators, and other interactive formats.

Core idea: engagement should support understanding. Humor, points, competition, surprise, and personalization are useful only when they help the viewer notice, interpret, or remember something meaningful.

Relevance and agency

These techniques make an interaction feel connected to the viewer or responsive to their choices.

Personal relevance

Personal relevance connects the topic to the viewer’s own location, habits, preferences, identity, or circumstances.

Make it apply to me

How it engages

The viewer has a reason to care because the result changes based on information they provide.

Works well with

Calculators, matchers, local maps, self-assessments, and personalized explainers.

Watch for

Ask only for information the story needs and explain how it affects the result.

Agency and consequence

Agency lets viewers make a meaningful choice. Consequence shows how that choice changes the outcome.

Choice changes result

How it engages

The viewer sees that their decision matters rather than merely advancing the interface.

Works well with

Branching paths, simulations, policy explainers, and decision-based stories.

Watch for

Choices should produce meaningfully different outcomes, not cosmetic variation.

Social comparison

Social comparison shows how a viewer’s response compares with responses from a larger group.

Compare with others

How it engages

The viewer becomes curious about whether their judgment is typical or unusual.

Works well with

Polls, predictions, opinion questions, and benchmark comparisons.

Watch for

Clearly identify where comparison data comes from. Do not present invented results as real.

Challenge

A challenge gives viewers a clear task that requires effort, judgment, observation, or problem solving.

Give viewers a task

How it engages

The viewer has a concrete goal and can test their skill or perception.

Works well with

Identification, sorting, spotting errors, drawing predictions, and simulations.

Watch for

The challenge should teach something, not merely make the task harder.

Collection and completion

Collection and completion encourage viewers to discover every item, finish a set, or reach a visible endpoint.

Complete the set

How it engages

Visible progress creates momentum and makes viewers curious about what remains.

Works well with

Explorable diagrams, maps, galleries, clue hunts, and multi-part explainers.

Watch for

Do not reward meaningless clicking. Each discovery should add useful information.

Curiosity and play

These techniques create an information gap, a playful framing, or an element of surprise.

Curiosity and delayed reveal

Delayed reveal creates an information gap by asking viewers to make a judgment before showing the answer.

Commit before reveal

How it engages

The act of guessing makes viewers more attentive to the eventual explanation.

Works well with

Quizzes, estimates, predictions, before-and-after comparisons, and data reveals.

Watch for

Do not delay information so long that the interaction feels obstructive.

Goofy comparison

A goofy comparison translates an abstract scale into something familiar, surprising, or humorous.

Playful scale

How it engages

Humor and familiarity make an otherwise abstract number easier to imagine.

Works well with

Calculators, generators, data stories, and explanatory result screens.

Watch for

The comparison must clarify the number. The joke should not overpower the information.

Randomness and surprise

Randomness introduces controlled variation so viewers can receive a different prompt, result, or example each time.

Try again

How it engages

Uncertainty and novelty encourage repeated interaction.

Works well with

Generators, scenario prompts, exploratory collections, and repeatable activities.

Watch for

Random results should still be meaningful and drawn from a carefully designed set.

Narrative framing and role-play

Narrative framing gives the viewer a role, situation, or objective that makes an interaction feel more coherent.

Give the viewer a role

How it engages

The viewer understands why they are making choices and what perspective they should adopt.

Works well with

Branching stories, simulations, decision trees, and scenario-based explainers.

Watch for

The role should clarify the issue rather than trivialize real people or consequences.
