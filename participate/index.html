Participate and Personalization

Participatory interactives ask viewers to put something into the story: a choice, a guess, an estimate, a drawing, a set of preferences, or a path through decisions. The result can be playful, explanatory, reflective, or personalized.

Core idea: participation should have a story purpose. A good participatory interactive does not only collect input; it uses that input to help viewers understand themselves, test an assumption, compare choices, or experience how a system responds.

Participatory interactives

Use these when the viewer’s input changes the result. The interaction may be simple and playful, but it should still explain something.

Viewer input generator

A generator asks viewers for a few inputs and returns a custom result. It works well for playful explainers, personalized recommendations, simple prototypes, and “what applies to me?” story moments.

Generated result

Story job

Best: Participate. Also useful for: Explain when the generated result teaches why the inputs matter.

Affordances

Participatory through viewer input; procedural because the result is generated; encyclopedic if the result pulls from a larger set of records or rules.

Agency

Limited personalization. The viewer chooses from designed inputs and receives a tailored result.

Possible interactions

  • Dropdowns, buttons, sliders, or short text inputs.
  • Generated result card with explanation, not just a label.
  • Reset or “try another combination” control.

Goofy comparison

A goofy comparison translates abstract scale into something more familiar, funny, or surprising. It is useful when numbers are too large, too small, or too abstract to feel meaningful.

Playful scale

Story job

Best: Participate and Explain. The viewer enters a familiar value and gets a comparison that makes scale easier to feel.

Affordances

Participatory through input; procedural through calculated comparison; experiential when the result changes how the scale feels.

Agency

Low-stakes play. The viewer personalizes the comparison without changing the underlying claim.

Possible interactions

  • Range slider for a familiar personal value.
  • Instant comparison text that updates on input.
  • Optional “show the math” reveal for transparency.

Quiz

A quiz asks viewers to commit to an answer before receiving feedback. It works best when the goal is to test recognition, surface misconceptions, or make viewers notice evidence before explanation.

Answer and feedback

Story job

Best: Participate. Also useful for: Reveal and Explain because feedback can teach the reason behind the answer.

Affordances

Participatory through choice; procedural through scoring and feedback; encyclopedic if feedback links to more context.

Agency

Guided challenge. The viewer chooses an answer from a controlled set.

Possible interactions

  • Multiple-choice buttons with immediate feedback.
  • Score tracking across questions.
  • Explanation after each answer, not only right/wrong.

Calculator

A calculator uses viewer input to produce a number, estimate, score, or category. It is strongest when the calculation helps the viewer understand a relationship or consequence.

Input to estimate

Story job

Best: Participate and Explain. The viewer sees how changing one value affects an outcome.

Affordances

Procedural because rules convert input into output; participatory through entered values; encyclopedic if it uses a dataset or lookup table.

Agency

Personalized exploration. The viewer can test “what if?” scenarios inside defined rules.

Possible interactions

  • Numeric inputs or sliders.
  • Live result with interpretation.
  • Scenario presets for quick comparison.

Matcher

A matcher pairs viewer preferences or observations with the closest item in a set. It works well for recommendations, field guides, “which one fits?” explainers, and simple classification prototypes.

Closest fit

Story job

Best: Participate. Also useful for: Explore because the result points the viewer into a collection.

Affordances

Encyclopedic because options come from a defined set; procedural because rules choose a match; participatory because the viewer supplies criteria.

Agency

Limited personalization. The viewer describes needs or observations and receives a match.

Possible interactions

  • Multi-step choices or compact filters.
  • Match result with explanation of why it matched.
  • Alternative matches or “close but not exact” feedback.

Draw to guess

A draw-to-guess interaction asks viewers to sketch, trace, or mark their prediction before showing the answer. It works best when spatial intuition is part of the learning.

Sketch before reveal

Story job

Best: Participate and Reveal. The viewer makes an estimate, then compares it to the answer.

Affordances

Participatory through drawing; spatial because the answer is location-based; procedural because the page records marks and reveals a reference layer.

Agency

Direct input. The viewer creates a visible guess rather than choosing from a fixed list.

Possible interactions

  • Canvas drawing or tap-to-place points.
  • Reveal reference route, boundary, or answer layer.
  • Clear/reset control for another attempt.

Paths

Use path patterns when the viewer’s choices change the sequence. These can feel like Twine, decision trees, or guided branching explainers.

Branching path

A branching path lets viewers move through a small choice structure. Each choice changes what they see next, creating a light story path or scenario simulation.

Choice structure

Story job

Best: Participate and Experience. Also useful for: Explain when choices demonstrate consequences.

Affordances

Procedural through branching logic; participatory through choices; experiential because the path changes how the viewer moves through the material.

Agency

Branching agency. The viewer controls the path, but the author controls possible branches and endings.

Possible interactions

  • Choice buttons that move to different nodes.
  • Back, restart, or path summary controls.
  • Consequence text that explains why a choice matters.

Decision tree

A decision tree walks viewers through a sequence of questions and uses their answers to produce an outcome. It is useful when the point is classification, triage, recommendation, or process guidance.

Structured choice

Story job

Best: Participate and Explain. It makes a process visible by letting viewers follow the logic.

Affordances

Procedural because rules determine the next question; participatory because answers steer the route; encyclopedic if outcomes link to more detail.

Agency

Guided path. The viewer answers questions, but the logic tree controls the structure.

Possible interactions

  • Yes/no or either/or questions.
  • Visible progress through the tree.
  • Outcome card with reasoning and next step.

Reflect, Guess, Estimate

Use these when the viewer should pause, commit to an idea, or compare their perception with an answer, benchmark, or recommendation.

Guess-then-reveal

A guess-then-reveal interaction asks viewers to make a prediction before showing the answer. It works because the act of guessing makes the reveal more meaningful.

Commit then reveal

Story job

Best: Participate and Reveal. Also useful for: Explain when the reveal corrects a misconception.

Affordances

Participatory through guessing; procedural through feedback; encyclopedic if the reveal opens more evidence.

Agency

Reflective micro-choice. The viewer commits to an answer, then compares it with the story’s evidence.

Possible interactions

  • Numeric entry, button choice, or slider guess.
  • Reveal button that compares guess to answer.
  • Short explanation of why the answer matters.

Prediction

A prediction interaction asks viewers what they expect to happen before showing an outcome. It is useful for patterns, change over time, risk, and cause-effect explanations.

Predict outcome

Story job

Best: Participate and Explain. The viewer sees whether their expectation matches the pattern.

Affordances

Participatory through prediction; procedural through result display; temporal when the prediction concerns change over time.

Agency

Reflective choice. The viewer selects a likely outcome and receives explanation.

Possible interactions

  • Prediction buttons or draggable forecast line.
  • Comparison of predicted result and actual result.
  • Follow-up explanation with context.

Self-assessment

A self-assessment asks viewers to reflect on their situation, habits, knowledge, or readiness and then returns a score or interpretation.

Reflective input

Story job

Best: Participate. Also useful for: Explain when the score teaches what factors matter.

Affordances

Participatory through checklist input; procedural through scoring; encyclopedic when each item links to advice or more context.

Agency

Personal reflection. The viewer evaluates their own situation and receives a tailored interpretation.

Possible interactions

  • Checklist, scale, or short-answer reflection.
  • Score with interpretation.
  • Recommended next steps based on responses.
